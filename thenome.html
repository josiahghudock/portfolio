About The 'Nome

The 'Nome is a free user-friendly online metronome with advanced capabilities. It is built specifically for practicing odd-time, polyrhythmic, odd-tuplet, or nested tuplet rhythms. Ultra-Nerd mode allows for "irrational" time signatures and extremely precise sequencing. If you found this tool useful, have questions, or suggestions please let me know!

The 'Nome — Guide & Examples

What is The 'Nome?

The 'Nome is a tool musicians can use to work on their timing. The metronome keeps perfect time. Musicians can compare their timing to the machine's or use it as a foundation for the construction of their own rhythms.

Is it Just Another Metronome?

No! The 'Nome takes many features available from different apps and combines it into one free user-friendly tool. Metronomes can be built for different purposes. The 'Nome takes a more mathematical approach to rhythm. It allows for precise timing controls for advanced musicians, including features for polyrhythms, nested tuplets, and irrational time signatures. In this regard, The 'Nome is unmatched in its technicality.

A traditional metronome sounds at every beat, but a beat sequencer like The 'Nome allows musicians to construct custom rhythmic sequences to use as a metronomic reference.

Using The 'Nome:

The Basics:

First, set your favorite BPM tempo. Hitting play, The 'Nome acts as a traditional metronome would, counting off bars of 4 at the chosen tempo.

To customize your metronome, you can toggle certain beats on or off by clicking on their spot on the circle. You can also adjust the number of beats, allowing for all X/4 standard time signatures.

If you want to adjust the pitch or timbre of the sound, you can do so in Sound Settings & Synthesis. You can also adjust the volume of each individual beat, for instance if you wanted an accent on the first beat.

Pro Tip If you ever get lost, the Reset 4/4 button will bring you right back to the beginning.

Swing:

Instead of playing strictly "on-the-grid", many musicians intentionally "swing" their rhythms by playing slightly before or after where a note should lie mathematically. The 'Nome is built for musicians who want to use a more precise swing through use of various tuplets. There are several degrees of swing that musicians can use in their performance. A nested tuplet is just "a tuplet within a tuplet". Technically speaking, most musicians are very comfortable with at least one type of nested tuplet. Indeed, sixteenth notes can be thought of as "4-lets" or equivalently as "2-lets" nested inside "2-lets". Likewise, sextuplets can be thought of as "3-lets" nested within "2-lets". Check it out here!:

The 'Nome allows for the construction of more complicated nested tuplets. To use it, first activate Custom Recursive Subdivisions. Above the circle, you can now switch between toggle mode (turning beats on or off) and subdivision mode. In subdivision mode, you can click the L1 box on any beat to select your desired subdivision of that beat. Going further, you can click the L2 box on any of those subdivisions to add a second layer of subdivision.

Polyrhythm:

Colloquially, a polyrhythm occurs when two different rhythms are played against each other. Strictly speaking, a x:y polyrhythm occurs when x beats are played against y beats, all equally spaced, and such that the two rhythms align on the first beat. The most popular polyrhythm is 3:2, the so-called "hemiola". The 'Nome allows for both polyrhythmic and polymetric constructions.

By activating polyrhythm and selecting a beat count, you can instantly superimpose any number of beats over your existing beat sequence.

Ultra-Nerd Mode:

Ultra-Nerd Mode extends the functionality of The 'Nome to allow for greater polyrhythmic abilities and use of non-dyadic time signatures. If you are in this deep, I'm just assuming you know what you're doing theoretically.

You can input your rhythm block by block in the following way: Set the duration in terms of "the time it takes to play y notes as r-lets" for parameters y and r. Then choose how many notes to squeeze into that duration (equal to y by default).

You can also choose to turn on the Beat Click, which is like the "meta-metronome" to your rhythm. Because this function allows for non-dyadic time signatures, the last beat of your pulse might be cut short, as expected.

Nested subdivisions are still available here, if you're brave...

In Closing

That's the gist of it! The possibilities are vast but not endless, though there is enough depth here you should hopefully be able to model a segment of almost any metric rhythm you might wish to perform. Happy practicing!
